# Competitor Research: Blockchain Development Services

**Target page:** https://clickmastersblockchaintechnologies.com/service/blockchain-development-services
**Research date:** 26 November 2026
**Method:** Live US web searches for the seed queries, followed by direct fetches of each competitor's ranking or primary service page. Headings, FAQs, CTAs, pricing and trust claims below were taken from those fetches. Every number a competitor publishes about itself is **self-reported and unverified**.

> **Limitations, stated plainly**
> - Search results came from a general web search tool, not a rank tracker. Order is "observed in results", not a verified Google position. No ranking positions are claimed here.
> - Several pages were long, and the fetch tool read the first ~40k characters verbatim and summarised the rest. Items from the summarised part are marked **[S]**.
> - Title tags were sometimes inferred from the first line of the page, not from raw HTML.
> - **4IRE** (4ire.net) failed DNS resolution, so there is no data for it.
> - **LeewayHertz**: `leewayhertz.com/blockchain-development-company/` currently serves an *Enterprise AI Development* page, not blockchain content. It was excluded as a blockchain competitor. That is itself a useful finding: a known brand has let its main blockchain URL drift off-topic.
> - **Appinventiv**: the guessed URL returned 404. Its blockchain page appeared in search (`appinventiv.com/?p=13097`) but was not analysed.

**What this tells us about intent**
- "Blockchain development services" and "blockchain development company" return a **mixed commercial SERP**: vendor service pages next to directories (Clutch, G2, Upwork, DesignRush). Searchers are comparing vendors. The page has to help someone shortlist, not just say "we do blockchain".
- "Blockchain development agency" is **dominated by directories**. It's a poor primary target. Use it as a natural variant at most.
- "Custom blockchain development" pulls in **educational guides** (build vs. buy, cost, roadmap). There's room for content that explains decisions, not only one that lists services.
- Toptal and ScienceSoft appear for nearly every query. Their pages are broad, long and full of trust signals.

## 3. Patterns across the competitor set

| Element | How common | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| H1 = "Blockchain development services" (exact or near) | 7 of 11 | The head term belongs in the H1. Exact-match alone doesn't set anyone apart. |
| Service card grid (8–16 items) | 11 of 11 | Universal and interchangeable. Buyers skim it. |
| Platform/tech list | 11 of 11 | Usually a logo wall. **Almost none explain *why* you'd pick one chain over another.** |
| Industry list | 10 of 11 | Usually a one-line description each. |
| Process section | 10 of 11 | 4–6 steps, generic. Few say what the client gets at each step. |

## 4. Opportunities for ClickMasters

1. **Be the page that helps a buyer decide, not just shortlist.** Give an honest "when blockchain fits / when it doesn't" filter, a network selection matrix (public L1, L2 rollup, appchain, permissioned), and a clear account of what drives cost and timeline. None of the 11 does all three.
2. **Show security in detail.** Name the practices: threat modelling, invariant and fuzz testing, static analysis, upgrade and admin-key design, multisig and timelocks, independent audit, monitoring. This answers the main worry of CTOs and fintech buyers.
3. **Don't compete on stat walls.** Competitors' numbers conflict and can't be verified. ClickMasters' own site labels its portfolio entries "illustrative". The new page should build trust from **specifics of method and deliverables**, and leave space for verified proof (see final-quality-review.md).
4. **Treat this page as a hub.** ClickMasters already has 11 dedicated service pages (smart contracts, DeFi, NFT, wallets, exchanges, enterprise, tokenization, Web3, GameFi, consulting, payment gateway). The main page should **summarise each with a clear link** instead of competing with them for their own keywords. Competitors like Peiko and PixelPlex do this.
5. **Cover post-launch operations and project takeover.** Almost no competitor covers upgrades, key custody, indexer/RPC reliability, or inheriting a half-built codebase. These are real buyer situations.
6. **Keep the heading structure tight.** SoluLab (19 H2s) and SpaceDev (two "why choose" sections) show how sprawl dilutes a page. Target 12–14 H2s, each with one job.
